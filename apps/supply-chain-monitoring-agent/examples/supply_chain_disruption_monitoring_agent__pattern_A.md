## BACKGROUND FRAMING

The Asia–Europe lane is operating inside a **compound crisis** that has no modern precedent: the Red Sea/Suez corridor remains effectively closed due to a simultaneous Houthi resumption of attacks and an Iran-driven Strait of Hormuz blockade; Cape of Good Hope routing has become structural rather than temporary; and European landside infrastructure is constrained by low Rhine water levels and recurring labor disputes. Against this backdrop, carrier capacity management and blank-sailing discipline are the primary tools holding rates above pre-crisis floors, even as Asia-Europe spot rates have been declining since July.

## OPERATIONAL GUIDANCE NOTES FOR PLANNERS

1. **Route arbitrage is live but fragile.** Some Gemini and CMA CGM services are now routing westbound via Suez (Zencargo, 22 Sept 2026). MSC only transits eastbound. Premier Alliance has no Suez services. Plan for split routing within any single alliance's schedule.
2. **Do not assume rate declines mean available space.** Carriers are maintaining 6% blank sailing rates to defend pricing; specific sailings can be very tight even when the overall market looks soft.
3. **Add 10–14 day buffers** on all Asia–Europe delivery commitments until Cape routing normalises or Suez fully reopens.
4. **European landside remains the weakest link.** Rotterdam APMT MVII at 85–90% utilisation and Hamburg clearing strike backlogs; build 2–3 days buffer on all hinterland connections. Use barge alternatives with caution given Rhine levels.
5. **Indian Subcontinent bookings** require close monitoring through October: Colombo at 130% utilisation, Nhava Sheva monsoon impacts, and empty equipment shortages persist across several carrier strings.
