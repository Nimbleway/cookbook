# Asia-Europe Container Shipping — Current Disruptions (as of 23 Sep 2026)

The Asia-to-Europe lane carries the bulk of eastbound containerised trade between Northeast/ Southeast Asia and North Europe, the UK, and the Mediterranean. Below are the five disruptions and one supporting item that materially affect this lane over the recent weeks, ranked by operational significance. Each carries a date and a cited source.

## 1. Gemini Cooperation step-up of Suez routings — partial unwind of Cape rerouting

- **Event:** Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd announced on **2026-09-15** that four more Gemini Cooperation services (AE5, AE11, AE12, ME2) will move back through the Red Sea / Suez Canal, joining AE15 and AE19 that already use the corridor [1]. AE11's first westbound sailing from Tanjung Pelepas under the new routing is **2026-09-19**; AE5 follows on **2026-09-21** and ME2 on **2026-09-24** [1].
- **Disruption type:** Rerouting / carrier network design.
- **Geography:** Red Sea, Suez Canal, Asia-to-Europe.
- **Lanes affected:** Asia-Northern Europe, Asia-Mediterranean, India-Europe. Ports called include London, Rotterdam, Algeciras, Barcelona, Genoa, Colombo [1].
- **Operational impact:** Potentially restores the shorter (~4-week shorter per Hapag-Lloyd spokesperson cited by Reuters via Trans.info [2]) Asia-Europe routing, releasing effective vessel capacity and easing schedule reliability. Carriers stress the move remains contingent on Red Sea security [1] [3].
- **Severity:** **Moderate (positive for capacity, high conditionality).** First Gemini Suez move was AE15/SE3 in July 2026 with *Majestic Maersk* as the first sailing [2] [3]. This is a phased expansion, not a wholesale return.

## 2. Houthi ground advance on Mokha and Perim Island — security spike at Bab el-Mandeb

- **Event:** The Iran-backed Houthis seized the Yemeni Red Sea port of Mokha on **2026-09-10** and reportedly advanced on Perim Island on **2026-09-11**; Perim sits inside the Bab el-Mandeb Strait linking the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden [4]. The group had previously declared a naval blockade on Saudi shipping in the Red Sea on 2026-07-20, struck a Saudi Bahri tanker in the Red Sea on 2026-08-24, and on 2026-08-12 killed at least six crew in a Bab el-Mandeb cargo-vessel strike plus a double-tap on rescuers [5] [6].
- **Disruption type:** Security (war risk).
- **Geography:** Southern Red Sea, Bab el-Mandeb Strait, Yemeni coastline near Yanbu/Saudi oil exports [4].
- **Lanes affected:** All Asia-Europe container transits currently routed via Suez (the four new Gemini services added 15 Sep + AE15/AE19) [1].
- **Operational impact:** Sharply raises the probability carriers either pause the new Gemini Suez rerouting or divert back to Cape of Good Hope. Drewry's 17 Sep WCI commentary explicitly flags "renewed security risks around the Red Sea and Bab el-Mandeb" as the swing factor on the pace of Suez restoration [7]. U.S. declined to widen strikes to the Houthis on 11 Sep [4].
- **Severity:** **High.** This is the directional event most likely to reverse Item 1.

## 3. Drewry World Container Index 17 Sep 2026 — Asia-Europe spot rates ease on returning Suez capacity

- **Event:** Drewry's WCI composite rose 1% to **$4,500/40ft** on **2026-09-17**, driven by Transpacific strength; the Asia-Europe lane weakened as Suez capacity returns. Carriers announced **9 blank sailings** for the week of 17 Sep (up from 8), with **4 blank sailings on Asia-Europe** alone (vs 1 the prior week) [7].
- **Key figures from the 17 Sep WCI:**
 - Shanghai → Rotterdam: **–9% to $3,626/40ft** [7]
 - Shanghai → Genoa: **–5% to $4,016/40ft** [7]
 - Shanghai → Los Angeles: **+5% to $7,712/40ft** (Golden Week / front-loading) [7]
 - Shanghai → New York: **+7% to $10,394/40ft** [7]
 - Shanghai waiting time rose from **65 hrs (Wk 36) to 78 hrs (Wk 37)** [7]
- **Disruption type:** Freight-rate movement (rate/volatility signal).
- **Geography:** Global with the Asia-Europe split dominant.
- **Lanes affected:** Asia-Northern Europe (declining), Asia-Mediterranean (declining), Transpacific (firming on capacity management ahead of China's Golden Week).
- **Operational impact:** Drewry explicitly expects Asia-Europe rates to "decline slightly next week" as Suez services return and demand stays weak [7]. Transpacific tightness adjacent to Asia-Europe rolls capacity across trades.
- **Severity:** **Moderate.** Directional change, not a shock — but the stronger Suez signal in the index is the quantifiable read of the Gemini moves in Item 1.

## 4. Shanghai + Ningbo berthing delays post-Typhoon Saudel — Asia load-port congestion at 10-day marks

- **Event:** Following Typhoon Saudel (closures 2026-08-26 to 29) and earlier storms Bavi and Dolphin, Shanghai Yangshan and Waigaoqiao plus Ningbo continued to record berthing delays into early September. The Loadstar reported on **2026-09-01** that berthing delays at Shanghai and Ningbo were hitting **10 days** [8]. Hapag-Lloyd's late-August ops update confirmed **5–7 day waits** for Gemini vessels >366 m at Shanghai Yangshan, **8–10 days** for smaller Gemini vessels, **7–11 days** for non-Gemini; Ningbo recorded **4–5 days** for Gemini and **3–6 days** for non-Gemini [9]. OOCL Logistics on **2026-08-19** cited Yangshan waits up to **12 days**, Waigaoqiao 7–8 days, Ningbo 3–5 days [9].

 Industry-wide contextualising figures (27 Aug): Linerlytica estimated **over 4.3 million TEU** was waiting to berth at container ports globally — roughly **12.6% of the global containership fleet** — exceeding the previous peak of ~4 million TEU recorded during Covid-era disruption [9]. Sea-Intelligence put **1.7 million TEU** in continuous delays (≈5% of global deepsea capacity, 2.8 pts above the pre-pandemic baseline) [9].
- **Disruption type:** Port congestion (weather-driven).
- **Geography:** East China coast, Shanghai Yangtze River Delta, Yangshan Island, Ningbo-Zhoushan.
- **Lanes affected:** Asia-Northern Europe, Asia-Mediterranean, intra-Asia, Transpacific (carrier rotations out of Shanghai).
- **Operational impact:** Ships miss berth windows; rolled cargo, equipment shortages, longer truck turnarounds and inland rail knock-on at the load end. Recovery windows between typhoons have been repeatedly compressed through this season — Xeneta's Peter Sand called it a "special typhoon season" for China's mega ports [9]. Backlogs requeue at destination ports and drive the blank sailings recorded in the Drewry WCI 17 Sep release [7].
- **Severity:** **High.** This is the load-end counterpart to European congestion and the most quantitatively documented disruption in the corpus.

## 5. German seaport labor action and follow-on Dutch strike threat — North Range schedule risk

- **Event:** Union ver.di ran a **24-hour warning strike** across Germany's six main container-seaport range on **2026-08-17 to 18**, hitting Hamburg, Bremerhaven, Wilhelmshaven, Bremen, Brake and Emden. Roughly **5,000 of ~11,000 workers** covered by the ZDS collective agreement joined, halting container handling at Hamburg's HHLA terminals (Altenwerder, Burchardkai, Tollerort) and EUROGATE [10]. The third round of negotiations opened in Hamburg on **2026-08-24** with no announced result by Aug 25, and a **48-hour strike** was reported by **2026-09-03** running through 2026-09-04 across the same German range, with planned strikes at **Rotterdam, Amsterdam and Zeeland** on 2026-09-04 cited by FMS Logistics [11]. The union's demand is **8.2% over 12 months** plus a €2.50/hr floor; the ZDS employers' offer is **5.1% over 19 months** [10]. Hamburger-era baseline wait into Hamburg of **up to 6 days** was reported by HMM via Lloyd's List [12]. Drewry's 17 Sep WCI note explicitly flags "potential German port strikes could worsen congestion and cause schedule disruptions in North Europe" [7].
- **Disruption type:** Labor (port-worker stoppages + threatened escalation).
- **Geography:** German North Sea range (Hamburg, Bremerhaven, Wilhelmshaven, Bremen, Brake, Emden); Dutch range (Rotterdam, Amsterdam, Zeeland).
- **Lanes affected:** Asia-Northern Europe / Asia-Mediterranean North-Europe calls. As of August, North Europe barges at Rotterdam/Antwerp were recording **72–96 hour waits**; Hamburg vessel queues were running **6 ships at anchor** [12].
- **Operational impact:** Inside the ZDS framework there is no domestic-German fallback port since Wilhelmshaven struck alongside Hamburg [10]. Practical substitution is discharge in Rotterdam/Antwerp plus barge/rail inland; carrier omissions so far (e.g., Maersk TA5, CMA CGM NeoSamba) demonstrate how carriers are pre-emptively shifting calls [12]. Containers bound for German inland destinations sit 2–4 days over their pre-strike window during requeueing.
- **Severity:** **Moderate.** Cyclical and well-flagged in advance by carriers; Antwerp berth waits rose from 32 hrs (Wk 13) to 44 hrs (Wk 20) earlier in 2026 [12]. A 24-hour strike rarely stays 24 hours downstream — but with the soft Asia-Europe spot near $4,700/40ft mid-to-late August [10], reroute costs generally exceed waiting costs for most cargo.

## Supporting item (lower significance) — Port of Tanjung Pelepas cyber incident

- **Event:** A cyber attack temporarily suspended container terminal operations at **Port of Tanjung Pelepas** (PTP, Malaysia) — a major Southeast Asian transhipment hub on the Asia-Europe corridor — with normal operations resuming by **2026-09-15** per industry updates [1] (listing PTP in related 15 Sep coverage).
- **Disruption type:** Operational / cyber.
- **Geography:** Strait of Malacca, Malaysia.
- **Lanes affected:** Asia-Europe (Gemini AE11 first westbound sailing departed Tanjung Pelepas on 2026-09-19 under the new Suez routing [1]).
- **Operational impact:** Limited, timing-sensitive — service now restored.
- **Severity:** **Low.** Watch-list item only, included for completeness given its proximity to the AE11 restart.

---

## Net read for the Asia-Europe lane right now

The dominant tension on the lane is between the **restoration** signal (Gemini Suez return from 15 Sep, Drewry's falling Asia-Europe spot rates, WCI composite $4,500/40ft in line with return-of-capacity) and the **deterioration** signal (Houthi ground advance on the Bab el-Mandeb from 10–11 Sep, plus Shanghai/Ningbo berthing delays stuck near 10 days throughout September). Carrier behaviour is the swing indicator: any reversal by Maersk or Hapag-Lloyd of the four new Gemini services back to the Cape will re-tighten effective Asia-Europe capacity and flip rates the other way. German port-strike risk and Netherlands follow-on action add a periodic European-leg overlay rather than a directional Asia-Europe shock.
